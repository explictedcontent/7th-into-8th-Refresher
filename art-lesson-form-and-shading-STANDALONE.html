🟡 Warm-up · Flat → Solid

Every solid object is built from a simple form. A flat shape is 2D (just an outline); a form is the same thing made 3D with shading. Match each flat shape to the form it becomes:

💡 The 5 parts of light on any object

Pick one light direction (say, top-left) and every round object shows these five things, always in the same order from bright to dark:

#PartWhat it is
1HighlightThe tiny brightest dot where light hits straight on
2MidtoneThe object's normal, medium color
3Core shadowThe darkest band, curving across the side away from the light
4Reflected lightA soft glow on the shadow edge, bounced up from the table
5Cast shadowThe dark shape the object throws onto the ground

🌑🌕 Value scale & light direction

A value scale is a strip that steps from white to black. Drawing one trains your hand to make smooth, believable shadows. In your head, read this 5-step scale from lightest to darkest:

  • ⚪ White
  • 🌑 Light gray
  • 🌘 Medium gray
  • 🌑 Dark gray
  • ⚫ Black

If the light comes from the top-left, the highlight sits on the side, and the core shadow + cast shadow fall toward the side. The shadow is always on the side .

🎡
This is where last lesson pays off: on the blending wheel, slide up for the highlight (a tint) and down for the core and cast shadows (shades) — same color, different value.

🖌️ Studio A · Shade a 3D sphere

Let's make a flat circle look like a real ball. Work in this order:

  • 1. Pick a color on the wheel and lightly fill a big circle (that's your midtone).
  • 2. Slide the side bar down for a darker shade and paint the core shadow as a curved band on the lower-right.
  • 3. Slide up for a light tint and dab a highlight on the upper-left.
  • 4. Switch to ink/black and paint a cast shadow on the ground, stretching to the lower-right.
  • 5. Save it when you're proud of it!

🍎 Studio B · A tiny still-life

Now use the same trick on a little scene. Draw two or three solid objects sitting on a table — maybe a ball, a box, and a cup — and give each one a highlight, a core shadow, and a cast shadow that all point the same direction (because there's one light). Matching shadow directions is the secret that makes a drawing look real.

📏
Composition tip — the rule of thirds: imagine the canvas split into 9 squares by two lines across and two down. Place your main object near a spot where the lines cross, not dead-center. It instantly looks more interesting.
