🌈 Color Theory Basics

The color wheel

  • Primary colors — red, yellow, blue. Can't be mixed from others.
  • Secondary colors — orange, green, purple. Mix two primaries.
  • Complementary colors sit opposite (like blue & orange). Side by side, they pop!

Warm vs. Cool

Warm 🔥 reds, oranges, yellows — cozy, exciting, close.
Cool ❄️ blues, greens, purples — calm, quiet, far away.

Use everything you learned. Follow the steps in your studio — take your time. 🎨

  2. Add a big round sun in a warm color (red, orange, yellow).

🧭 Part 2: Order of Operations (PEMDAS)

  1. Parentheses first
  2. Exponents
  3. Multiply & Divide — left to right
  4. Add & Subtract — left to right
⚠️
Watch out! Multiply and divide are a team — do whichever comes first, left to right. Same for add and subtract.
Worked example

Solve 6 + 2 × (5 − 1)²

  1. Parentheses: 5 − 1 = 4 → 6 + 2 × 4²
  2. Exponent: 4² = 16 → 6 + 2 × 16
  3. Multiply: 2 × 16 = 32 → 6 + 32
  4. Add: 6 + 32 = 38

🏗️ Building a Complete Sentence

Subject + Predicate

Subject = who/what it's about. Predicate = what they do or are (has the verb).

"My older sister paints every weekend."

ProblemWhat it isFix
FragmentMissing a subject or verb. "Because she loves art.""She joined the club because she loves art."
Run-onTwo sentences jammed together."She paints, and she also sketches."

🔁 The Scientific Method

  1. Question — What are you curious about?
  2. Research — Find out what's already known.
  3. Hypothesis — A testable "If… then…" prediction.
  4. Experiment — Test it, changing only one thing.
  5. Analyze — Collect data, look for patterns.
  6. Conclusion — Was your hypothesis supported?
💡
A good hypothesis is testable. "If I play music for my plant, then it will grow taller than one with no music."

🧪 Lab Safety Rules

  • Wear safety goggles when told to
  • Never eat or drink in the lab
  • Tie back long hair and roll up loose sleeves
  • Tell your teacher about any spill right away
  • Know where the sink, fire extinguisher, and exit are
  • Never mix chemicals unless instructed
⚠️
Golden rule: when in doubt, ask the teacher before you act.

🧬 How the Body Is Built

Your body is organized from the tiniest pieces all the way up to the whole you:

  1. Cells — the smallest living building blocks (you have trillions!)
  2. Tissues — groups of similar cells working together (like muscle tissue)
  3. Organs — different tissues teamed up for a job (like the heart)
  4. Organ systems — organs working together (like the circulatory system)
  5. Organism — all the systems together = the whole body (you!)
💡
Smallest → biggest: Cells → Tissues → Organs → Systems → Organism.

📚 Why Geography Matters

Long before phones could show a blue dot on a map, explorers crossed oceans using only the stars, a compass, and lines of latitude and longitude. Knowing exactly where you are has always shaped human history — where cities grew, which countries traded, and how armies moved.

Geography still shapes daily life. Mountains and rivers decide where roads and farms can go. A country near the Equator has a very different climate from one near the poles. When you understand maps, news about faraway places makes a lot more sense.
